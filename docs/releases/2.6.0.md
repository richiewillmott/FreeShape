# FreeShape 2.6.0

Selecting in the feature list and the parts list now works as in Onshape; Check interference for parts; Fill and
Constrained surface; and renaming a variable anywhere follows it everywhere.

## Highlights

**Clicks add to the selection** — in the feature list and the parts list, each click picks one more: click
Part 1, then Part 2, and both are picked, ready for a Boolean, a Compound or a right-click menu. Before, the
second click replaced the first. Click a picked one again to drop it; Shift+click still picks a run, and `Esc` or
a click on empty space clears the lot.

**The parts list and the view agree** — the parts picked in the list are the parts picked in the 3D view, and
the other way round: pick a part in the view and it's highlighted in the list. Faces, edges and planes you've
already picked stay picked when you add parts from the list. Top, Front and Right clicked in the feature list are
picked in the view as well.

**The feature list and the view, like Onshape** — click a feature and what it made is shown in the same orange
as geometry you pick (not a separate tint); pick a face or edge in the view and the feature that made it is marked
in the list (click a fillet's round face and *Fillet 1* lights up, click the Front plane and *Front* does); and a click on empty space, or `Esc`,
deselects a feature too.

**Check interference** — select two or more parts, right-click ▸ *Check interference*: where any of them
overlap is shown in red, with each pair and its overlap volume listed, until the model next changes.

**Fill** — a surface patch over a closed loop of edges and sketch curves: an open end of a surface body, a hole,
a sketched outline. *Position* just meets the surfaces around it, *Tangent* meets them smoothly, and points you
pick shape it. *Add* sews the patch into the surface it closes, so a surface body can be closed up and turned into
a solid. (Curvature continuity and guide curves aren't there yet.)

**Constrained surface** — a smooth surface fitted through points (vertices, sketch points), or through every
vertex of a part (a point cloud or scan imported as STEP or BREP), within a tolerance you set.

**Fillet radius arrow** — drag the arrow on the round to set the radius, as in Onshape (in for bigger). Too big to make? The round
is drawn in purple and its edges in red, so you see where it runs into trouble, not just a message.

**Chamfer: Tangent propagation** — a checkbox, as in Onshape: on (the default), the bevel follows smooth chains
round fillets; off, it bevels just the edges you picked, at any size. Older chamfers work as they did.

**Fillets into a rounded corner** — fillet an edge that runs into a corner an earlier fillet rounded, at that
fillet's radius or bigger (it used to stop just under it): the two are rounded together so the corner blends, as
Onshape's edge overflow does, and they stay separate features.

**Variables follow a rename** — rename a variable in its own dialog, or its name in the feature list, and every
feature, sketch dimension and configuration using it follows, in one undo step (it used to break them all with
*Unknown variable*). And value boxes now offer variables without the `#`: type `w` and pick `#width`.

## Smaller things

- **Take one pick out of a selection box** — each edge, face or part in a feature dialog's box has its own × at
  the end of its row (red under the mouse), as in Onshape; `Delete` removes the highlighted one.
- **Cleaner ✓ and ✕** on feature dialogs — drawn icons with a soft round highlight, instead of font characters
  that looked different on every screen.
- **Fixed:** the feature you're editing is shaded in the feature list again.

## Download

- **Windows:** `FreeShape-Setup-2.6.0.exe` — installs over 2.5 (or the portable zip). SmartScreen may warn because
  the installer isn't code-signed yet: **More info → Run anyway**.
- **Mac (Apple Silicon):** `FreeShape-2.6.0-mac-arm64.dmg` — drag into Applications; the first time,
  right-click → **Open** → **Open**.
- **Linux:** `FreeShape-2.6.0-x86_64.AppImage` — `chmod +x` it and run it.

## Upgrading

From 2.5: install over it; nothing in your files changes.

From 2.4 or earlier: read [Upgrading from 2.4](2.5.0.md#upgrading-from-24) in the 2.5.0 notes first. 2.5 moved
Part Studios to a new file format; older files convert by themselves when they open.
